## Caption (EN)

The 50-year-old who takes creatine and never lifts a weight — does it do anything?
Evidence-based training, nutrition and supplements.

A 2026 trial at Texas A&M put 64 sedentary adults aged 45–65 on 10 g of creatine a day for 12 weeks, with and without training.

Without the gym, DXA lean tissue went up 1.1 kg while both placebo groups did not move. But DXA counts muscle plus water, creatine pulls water into muscle, and that group also ate more carbohydrate — so part of that kilogram is not new muscle.

Body fat is the clean part: creatine alone changed nothing (−0.42 points, same as placebo). The −3.24 points came only from lifting plus a calorie deficit.

The memory claim is the thinnest. No overall difference across the test battery; the one positive at week 6 was gone by week 12.

And creatinine rising on your blood work is arithmetic, not kidney damage — creatine degrades into creatinine. Just tell whoever reads your labs.

Chun et al. 2026, J Int Soc Sports Nutr (PMID 42578920).
